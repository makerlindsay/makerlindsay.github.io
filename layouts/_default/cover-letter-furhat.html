Dear Furhat team,

The first time a robot responds to the person who built it, something changes. Since 2014, I have been creating that moment for children, teachers and creators, and I am applying for the Growth and Community Lead role at Furhat Robotics to create it for the researchers and innovators shaping how people and machines interact. At the Children's Creativity Museum in San Francisco, and later at Strawbees in Sweden, I taught people to turn cardboard, servo motors and a few lines of code into robots that move, react and express themselves.

My work has always started with lowering the barrier to building. At Strawbees, I authored more than 100 platform resources, including coding and robotics guides for Arduino, MakeCode and Python, video tutorials and lesson plans. I also pointed builders to a library of code examples, so they could start from something that works and make it their own. At international conferences such as ISTE, BETT, SETT, TCEA and EduTech Asia, I led talks and workshops on the programming potential of social and emotional robots, showing how robots see, think, react and even feel. A button press becomes the motion of a motor, and a simple build becomes a character that responds to the world around it.

I have spent almost a decade in hardware startups. I joined Strawbees as an early hire, built the educator community function from zero, and grew into Head of Education, wearing many hats across community, content, product and partnerships. I created products for new markets, from a storybook series for South Korea to US go-to-market launches. I worked with B2B partners to localize tools and resources so they could reach their own customers. I also brought teacher feedback into our learning platform, and gave sales and marketing the stories and resources they needed to reach schools.

Researchers and innovation teams discover Furhat through conferences, research papers, colleagues, workshops and community conversations, often over months. I know how to build that journey. As a learning designer at Strawbees, I connected teachers and makers learning robotics with the tools and support to bring it into their classrooms, and worked with researchers in programming and engineering. I grew a community of 637 educators, hosted a monthly webinar series, and scaled an ambassador program across 10+ countries, where members ran their own workshops and brought in their colleagues. At Furhat, I would do the same for your users, turning researchers into advocates and advocates into ambassadors who share what they build.

In my first months, I would talk to customers about how they found Furhat. I would run practical workshops where researchers build their first interactions, and create a starter library of examples that make the platform easy to try. I am eager to connect community work more closely to measurable growth alongside your marketing team, doubling down on what brings in new customers.

I am based in Stockholm, and critical thinking, creativity and collaboration guide everything I build. I would love to talk about how I can help grow the Furhat community.

Warm regards,

Lindsay Balfour
